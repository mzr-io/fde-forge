# Assessment Playbook

## Purpose

Run a fair, adaptive interview that reveals how a candidate behaves when business context is incomplete and the answer is not supplied.

## Interview structure

### 1. Orientation

Explain:
- The exercise is developmental and evidence-based.
- There may be incomplete or contradictory information.
- The candidate may ask questions, state assumptions and request evidence.
- There is no expectation that they know every industry.
- They should explain their reasoning and what they would validate.

Do not reveal scoring anchors during the live assessment unless the programme explicitly requires transparency.

### 2. Candidate profile

Ask conversationally, one question at a time. Avoid turning this into a long form. Collect only relevant professional context.

### 3. Scenario discovery

Give a short scenario and wait. Example:

"A distribution company says it is missing delivery commitments and asks you to help. They have not defined a software requirement."

Let the candidate choose what to do first.

### 4. Probe the reasoning

Select follow-ups based on the response. Useful probe families:

**Discovery**
- What do you need to understand before proposing anything?
- Whose work would you observe?
- How would you find out what actually happens rather than what the SOP says?
- What would you do if stakeholders describe different problems?

**Business**
- Which business outcome matters?
- How would you estimate the cost of this problem?
- Who benefits and who bears the cost of a change?
- What would make the investment worthwhile?

**Evidence**
- What data would you request?
- How would you test data completeness and accuracy?
- Can you trace one real case end-to-end?
- What observation would disprove your hypothesis?

**Root cause**
- What are the competing explanations?
- Which one would you test first and why?
- What would you sample?
- How would you distinguish correlation from cause?

**Solution**
- What is the smallest safe intervention?
- Could an SOP or process change solve this without software?
- What alternatives did you consider?
- What is the failure mode of your proposed solution?

**Execution**
- What would you prototype first?
- How would you integrate with existing systems?
- How would you test in a live operational environment?
- What is your rollback or fallback plan?

**Adoption**
- Who must change their behavior?
- What if supervisors bypass the system?
- How would you identify friction rather than blame users?
- How would you measure actual adoption?

**Impact**
- What baseline will you establish?
- Which KPIs will change if the intervention works?
- How will you attribute the change?
- What would you do if the expected benefit does not appear?

### 5. Introduce evidence progressively

Reveal new facts when the candidate asks relevant questions or when it is useful to test adaptability. Do not make the interview a guessing game. If the candidate requests information that a real FDE could reasonably obtain, provide it.

Use:
- Contradictory stakeholder accounts.
- Missing or unreliable records.
- A constraint that changes the solution.
- A new cost or risk.
- An operational exception.
- A failed first hypothesis.

### 6. Test practical output

At least one exercise should require an artifact or structured work product, such as:
- Process map.
- Hypothesis/evidence table.
- Transaction reconciliation.
- Prioritized opportunity list.
- Solution options and trade-offs.
- Lightweight prototype or technical plan.
- KPI baseline and measurement plan.

Adapt to the candidate's role. A frontend engineer need not demonstrate deep infrastructure expertise in every exercise, but must show the ability to collaborate and reason about the end-to-end solution.

### 7. Close and score

Do not give a score after every answer. Complete enough evidence first. Then provide:
- Strongest demonstrated behaviors.
- Weakest demonstrated behaviors.
- Areas not assessed.
- Competency ratings and evidence.
- Confidence and limitations.
- Development priorities.

## Interview integrity

- Use the same broad competencies and scoring anchors across candidates.
- Rotate scenario details to reduce answer memorization.
- Allow reasonable accommodations.
- Do not use irrelevant personal questions.
- Do not score accent, extroversion or presentation polish as a proxy for stakeholder skill.
- Assess communication by clarity, listening, adaptation and ability to build shared understanding.
- For hiring decisions, require human review and job-related work samples.
